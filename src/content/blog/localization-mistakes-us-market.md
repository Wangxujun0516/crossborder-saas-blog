Chinese SaaS companies expanding to the US often invest heavily in English translation but overlook deeper localization. Here are the most common mistakes we've observed.

## 1. Pricing that signals "cheap"

In China, competitive pricing is a virtue. In the US B2B market, **pricing that's too low signals lack of reliability**. US buyers associate price with quality, especially for enterprise software.

**Fix:** Price at parity with comparable US tools. Offer a free trial instead of a cheap plan.

## 2. Ignoring compliance badges

US enterprise buyers expect SOC 2 Type II, GDPR compliance, and often HIPAA. Chinese SaaS companies frequently skip these certifications early, which kills deals in procurement review.

**Fix:** Start the SOC 2 process before entering the US market. It takes 6-12 months.

## 3. English-only support

Providing support only in English (or worse, only in Chinese with machine translation) creates trust issues. US buyers expect responsive, native-language support during their business hours.

**Fix:** Hire US-based customer success managers, not just translators.

## 4. Overloading the UI

Chinese SaaS products tend to pack more features into a single screen. US users prefer cleaner interfaces with progressive disclosure.

**Fix:** Invest in a dedicated UX redesign for Western markets. Don't just translate the Chinese UI.

## 5. Wrong payment gateways

Alipay and WeChat Pay are ubiquitous in China but almost unused in US B2B. Requiring these for payment will lose you customers.

**Fix:** Integrate Stripe, offer credit card payments, and support net-30 invoicing for enterprise clients.

## Summary

Successful localization goes beyond translation. It's about adapting your pricing, compliance, support, UX, and payments to match US market expectations — while keeping the product strengths that made you successful in China.
